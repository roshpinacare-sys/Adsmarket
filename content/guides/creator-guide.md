# The creator's guide: publishing and earning on Steem, Hive, and Blurt without selling your soul

Written from measured experience: our own fleet publishes on these chains daily through gated pipelines, and this is what the reward mechanics actually respond to. No get-rich framing. These chains pay for attention earned honestly, and they are very good at detecting the alternative.

## How the money actually works

- Your post enters a **payout window** (7 days on Steem and Hive; Blurt settles much faster). During that window, votes determine the payout. After it closes, the amount is fixed and split (on Steem/Hive roughly half to you, half to the power you hold; liquid vs powered varies by chain settings).
- **Votes are weighted by stake**, not by headcount. One veteran whale outranks fifty fresh accounts. This is why early votes matter enormously and why relationships with curators beat follower counts.
- **Curation rewards** pay voters who find good posts early. This is the engine you should understand: curators are not charities, they are hunters of value, and a post that rewards them for finding it gets found.
- Nothing here is yield. Payouts fluctuate with token price and network activity. Anyone promising fixed returns from posting is selling something.

## What earns (measured, not vibes)

1. **Specificity.** "I ran the numbers: a 247-byte custom_json costs about 1.36 billion RC, and the account regenerates its pool every 5 days" beats "gas fees are low on these chains" every single time. Numbers are anchors for votes because they are rare and checkable.
2. **A real moment.** "At 23:58 the process died. At 23:59 it was back, without anyone touching it." Time-stamped reality reads like nothing else in a feed of abstractions.
3. **Usefulness with a takeaway.** The reader should finish with something they can do: a command, a checklist, a mistake to avoid.
4. **Consistency over bursts.** The payout window rewards accounts curators recognize. One strong post per day beats seven on Sunday and silence after.
5. **Voice.** Pick a lane and keep it. An account that posts photography Monday and gas economics Tuesday reads as compromised, and veterans can smell a repurposed account.

## What kills rewards

1. **Duplicate content.** The same article on three chains, or five near-identical posts on one account. Communities flag it, curators blacklist it, and the algorithmic feed bury it. Adapt the angle per network or stagger by days.
2. **Tag stuffing.** First tag is the post's permanent category. One umbrella tag (steem / hive / blurt), one or two precise niche tags, optionally one community tag. Five random tags is a spam signature.
3. **Begging.** "Upvote me," "resteem for resteem," vote-begging in comments. Instant reputation damage with the exact people who decide your payouts.
4. **AI slop.** These communities have developed antibodies. The tells: em dashes everywhere, "in today's fast-paced world," triple-point lists in every section, summaries that restate the post. If your draft has any of these, rewrite or don't post.
5. **Clickbait titles.** The title is the whole CTR, and a lying title burns reputation the seven-day window cannot repay. Curiosity without deception.
6. **Editing after payout.** Fixing errors inside the window with a visible edit note is respected. Rewriting history after the money settled is remembered.

## The first paragraph rule

On every frontend, the first paragraph becomes the preview in feeds. Topic plus value in two sentences. No warm-up laps, no "Hello everyone, today I want to talk about." Start mid-substance: "A 247-byte memo costs about 1.36 billion RC. Here is how to check that yourself."

## The curation side (where steady money actually is)

Posting is the visible half. The measured half: on these chains, **thoughtful curation often outearns posting** for consistent accounts, because curator rewards compound on every good post you find early, not just your own.

- Follow 5 to 10 accounts whose taste you trust and read what they vote before the crowd arrives.
- Comment with substance: add a number, a correction, an edge case. A comment that teaches is a reputation asset; "great post" is noise.
- Keep vote weight sane (we cap ours at 20% per vote). Maxing every vote burns mana and trains curators to see you as a bidder, not a reader.
- Blurt has no downvotes, which changes the game: nobody can punish a post, so readers punish by ignoring. Quality filtering moved into human attention, which makes genuine comments worth more there, not less.

## A realistic first month

- Week 1: one post daily in one lane, five substantive comments daily. No expectations. You are calibrating voice and learning who curates your niche.
- Week 2: note which posts got early curator attention and why. Double down on that format. Start engaging the curators' own threads.
- Week 3: your first payout lands (7-day window from week 1). It will be small. The number that matters is trend, not total.
- Week 4: decide your sustainable cadence. Most accounts that quit do it here, right before compounding starts.

## The honest bottom line

These chains pay real money for real attention, slowly, with a seven-day feedback loop that punishes fakes and rewards patience. If you want a casino, there are other chains. If you want to build a publishing reputation that is verifiable forever (every post you ever wrote, immutably on a public ledger, including the ones you wish you could delete), there is nowhere else like this. Write accordingly.
